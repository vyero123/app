Three ways to tell the sides apart

Same position, same influence state, same size — the only thing that changes between the three is the piece treatment. Squares are 42px, which is what they actually measure on a 375px phone. All three look fine at twice this size, which is why they are not shown at twice this size.

The position is the Immortal Game after 23.Be7#. Stress cases in it: a1 — Black piece, nobody controls the square · d3 — White piece on White ×2 amber · h5 — Contested 2–1.

A · Value inversion with keylines

White is near-white with a dark keyline; Black near-black with a light one. Each piece carries both values at its edge. This is what ships today.

The renders split the verdict, so here it is straight. In daylight B is the strongest — hollow versus solid reads instantly, and because both armies use one ink, legibility never depends on what is underneath. In shadow mode C is the strongest, and by a wide margin: a filled cream disc against a filled slate disc is read at a glance, where A's dark ring recedes into dark tints and B's double hairline makes you count lines.

My pick: B for the pieces, with C's filled disc for the shadow-mode ring. That takes each option where it is strongest. It costs two things worth knowing: White's army reads lighter in weight than Black's, and the disc masks the middle of every occupied square, so on those 23 squares you judge the influence intensity from the border rather than the whole tile.

If you want one option unmixed, take B. A is the conventional answer but it is also the one you said was too weak, so recommending it back to you would be no answer at all. C unmixed makes every piece look the same and turns the board into a field of discs — it identifies the side well and shows the piece badly. Nothing is shipped; say which and I will build it.
